# California Class II Whitewater Runs — Comprehensive List & Classics

> **Companion to the Class III, IV, and V documents.** Same methodology, same sources, same confidence-scoring approach. Class II occupies a different niche: it's where you take new paddlers, plan scenic overnights, and find reliable warm-weather floats. The "classic" bar here is less about difficulty and more about scenery, accessibility, and experience quality.

## Methodology

Same three-pass approach as the higher-class documents:

**1. Database enumeration.** Filtered the riverfacts.com / Holbek-Stanley database (~267 CA runs) for any run whose rating is predominantly II: "II", "II+", "I-II", "II-III" (where II is the modal character). Also included runs that appear in the III list but were downgraded or characterized as "really a II" during cross-referencing (e.g., Mokelumne Electra at normal flows).

**2. Editorial curation.** Cross-referenced against:
- American Whitewater river detail pages
- cacreeks.com (Bill Tuthill)
- Commercial outfitter "family" and "beginner" trip rosters
- Whitewater Guidebook (whitewaterguidebook.com)
- Local club beginner-trip lists (Gold Country Paddlers, Sacramento club write-ups)
- Dreamflows cross-listing

**3. Community signal proxy.** Runs that are the default "intro to whitewater" or "scenic overnight" in their region. Often the answer to "where should I take a friend who's never kayaked?" or "where can I go this weekend without committing to Class III?"

A run earned the **"classic"** label if it scored across at least two of those three dimensions AND is predominantly Class II at normal flows.

One definitional note: Class II has a character split similar to the IV doc's "sport vs. committing" distinction:
- **"Mellow II"** — wide channels, gentle riffles, occasional eddies. Suitable for open canoes and inflatable kayaks. Lower American, Klamath Upper.
- **"Sporty II" / "II+"** — more current, some maneuvering required, occasional small waves and features. Easy for an experienced paddler but a real learning experience for a beginner. EF Carson, Truckee town sections.
- **"II with teeth"** — rated II but has cold water, remote setting, or wood hazards that raise the consequence level beyond the rapid difficulty. Eel sections, Upper Sac calmer stretches.

## Refined "classics" — cross-source agreement

Runs appearing in at least two of {commercial/club beginner roster, editorial mention, community staple} AND predominantly II at normal flows.

### Tier 1 — iconic / must-do for any California Class II paddler

1. **Lower American, Nimbus Dam → Discovery Park** — Sacramento's home river. I-II, 23 miles. The most-paddled water in the Sacramento region, period. Year-round flows from Folsom Dam. Wide, friendly, with enough riffles to feel like you're on a river. Where every Sac-area paddler learns. Not exciting at your level, but you'll end up here with friends who are new to boats.

2. **East Fork Carson, Hangman's → CA-NV border (overnight)** — 20 miles, Class II with III pulses. Already a Tier 1 III classic, but the overnight experience is fundamentally Class II in character — long mellow stretches, hot springs, desert canyon scenery. You've done this. It remains one of the best multi-day river trips in the Sierra regardless of difficulty level.

3. **Klamath, Iron Gate Dam → Sarah Totten CG** — 48 miles, Class II. The premier Northern California multi-day float. Big-volume river through volcanic canyon, excellent camping, reliable summer flows. 2-4 day trip. Accessible to open canoes and beginning kayakers.

4. **Trinity, Lewiston Dam → Junction City** — 30 miles, Class II (with a few III rapids). Dam-released, reliable flows spring through fall. Gorgeous scenery, good camping, long enough for multi-day. A few rapids keep it interesting but the character is mellow. Strong candidate for the best "introduce someone to river camping" trip in NorCal.

5. **Mokelumne, Electra Run (Ponderosa Way → Hwy 49)** — 6 miles, Class II at normal flows. Closest dam-released run to Sacramento/Rocklin for quick after-work or weekend sessions. The default "warm-up" or "bring a new friend" run for the Sac paddling community. Bumps to II+/III above 1500 cfs.

6. **Lower Kern, Isabella → Sandy Flat (town run)** — 7 miles, II-III. Southern California's most accessible dam-released whitewater. Summer staple. Predominantly II with a few III drops. Where SoCal paddlers learn.

7. **Sacramento, Redding → Anderson (Lower Sac)** — 12 miles, Class II. Big-volume tailwater float. Famous as trout water, but also excellent as a scenic Class II paddle with cold, clear water below Shasta Dam. Year-round flows.

8. **Russian River, Cloverdale → Healdsburg** — 25 miles, Class II. The Bay Area's winter/spring river trip. Wine country scenery, mild rapids, good for canoes and kayaks alike. Rain-dependent season.

### Tier 2 — well-established regional classics

9. **Stanislaus, Camp Nine → Parrotts Ferry** — Classic Gold Country II. Limestone canyon, clear water, dam-controlled. Popular with families and canoeists. Pool level varies with New Melones Reservoir.

10. **Trinity, Junction City → Burnt Ranch (upper sections)** — Extends the Lewiston float downstream. Character remains mostly II before the canyon steepens toward Burnt Ranch Gorge. Good for multi-day extensions.

11. **Klamath, Sarah Totten → Happy Camp** — 36 miles, II-III. Continuation of the Iron Gate float. Picks up slightly in difficulty — transitions from pure II to borderline III in spots. Multi-day.

12. **Truckee, Tahoe City → River Ranch** — 5 miles, Class II. Short, scenic, convenient. The default "quick paddle near Tahoe" run. Summer dam releases from Lake Tahoe.

13. **Cache Creek, Hwy 20 → Bear Creek (upper section)** — 18 miles, II(III). The calmer section above Rumsey Canyon. Winter/spring rain-dependent. Sacramento/Bay Area within-reach.

14. **Smith River, Jed Smith SP → mouth** — I-II, 5 miles. The clearest water in California. Worth paddling for the scenery alone even though the rapids are trivial. Winter/spring flows.

15. **Cosumnes River, Michigan Bar → Hwy 99** — 12 miles, Class II. Sacramento foothills rain-dependent run. Narrow, tree-lined, intimate character. Short season but very close to home for Rocklin.

16. **SF Eel, Leggett → Piercy** — Class II section of the South Fork Eel. Redwood scenery. Rain-dependent, winter/spring only.

### Tier 3 — local / situational classics (less universal fame)

- **Bear River, Dog Bar → Camp Far West** — Gold Country local run. Short season.
- **Putah Creek, below Monticello Dam** — Quiet, cold, scenic. Near Davis/Sacramento.
- **West Carson, Hwy 4 → Woodfords** — Eastern Sierra, short, scenic.
- **Owens River, Pleasant Valley → Bishop** — Eastern Sierra desert float. Mellow, scenic, year-round.
- **MF Feather, Sloat → Nelson Point** — Remote. Mostly II with some III. Pretty canyon.
- **Van Duzen, Grizzly Creek section** — North Coast rain-dependent. Short, fun.
- **Walker River, main stem** — Eastern Sierra. Less-known, mellow.
- **Feather, Oroville → Thermalito** — Flatwater-ish, but accessible.

## What makes a Class II run "classic"?

The criteria are genuinely different from the III-V docs. At Class II, nobody is seeking out the hardest rapid. The value proposition is:

1. **Scenery and setting** — hot springs, canyons, redwoods, clear water
2. **Multi-day potential** — long enough to camp, remote enough to feel wild
3. **Accessibility** — reliable flows, easy shuttle, close to population centers
4. **Social utility** — the run where you bring new paddlers, family, or friends from out of town
5. **Reliability** — dam-released beats rain-dependent at this level, because you're often planning around other people's schedules

This is why the EF Carson overnight, Lower American, and Mokelumne Electra are Tier 1 — they serve these functions better than any other runs in their regions.

## Confidence notes

### Highest confidence (unambiguous, multi-source agreement)
Lower American, EF Carson overnight, Klamath Iron Gate, Trinity Lewiston, Mokelumne Electra, Lower Kern town run, Lower Sac Redding, Russian River. These are bedrock.

### Moderate confidence
- **Stanislaus Camp Nine** — pool level and reservoir management affect accessibility. Character is definitely II but access varies.
- **Cache Creek upper** — some sources lump it with the III-rated Rumsey section. At lower flows the upper is solidly II.
- **Smith River lower** — barely qualifies as II; it's almost flat. Included for scenery value.
- **Truckee town sections** — rating depends heavily on which stretch and flow level.

### Low confidence
- Many Class II runs are **under-documented** in the kayaker literature. Holbek/Stanley didn't obsess over cataloging II runs the way they did III-V. This inventory is less complete than the higher-class docs.
- Rain-dependent coastal II runs (Cosumnes, Russian, Eel sections) have variable character — a "II" at moderate flows can be a "III" at high water and a "I" at low water.
- SoCal coverage is thin. There are almost certainly Class II runs in the Transverse Ranges and coastal drainages that aren't in the riverfacts database.
- Access and permit situations are not verified. Some Lower American sections require day-use parking fees; Klamath has tribal land considerations.

### What I did NOT do
- Current flow windows per run
- Safety verification (wood status, current hazards)
- Put-in/take-out GPS coordinates
- Drive time from Rocklin
- Cross-reference with canoe-specific resources (Class II is where canoe and kayak lists diverge most)

## Proximity note for Rocklin-based paddlers

Class II is the "always available" tier. Within 90 minutes of Rocklin:
- **Lower American** — 30 min. Year-round.
- **Mokelumne Electra** — 75 min. Dam-released, most of the year.
- **Cosumnes** — 60 min. Winter/spring only.
- **Bear River** — 45 min. Short season.

Within 2-3 hours:
- **EF Carson** — 2.5 hrs. Spring/summer.
- **Truckee** — 2 hrs. Summer.
- **Stanislaus Camp Nine** — 2 hrs. Dam-controlled.
- **Cache Creek** — 2 hrs. Winter/spring.

This makes Class II the category where Rocklin geography shines — you have more options within day-trip range than almost any other home base in the state.
