# California Class III Whitewater Runs — Comprehensive List & Classics

> **Revision note (v2):** This doc has been cross-referenced against American Whitewater river pages, Whitewater Guidebook, cacreeks.com (Bill Tuthill), Dreamflows, awetstate.com, and commercial outfitter descriptions. See "Cross-reference corrections" section below for the deltas from v1.

---

## Cross-reference corrections (v1 → v2)

Cross-checks against AW, cacreeks, outfitter pages, and current local guides produced five real changes:

1. **NF Feather Tobin — removed from Class III classics.** The riverfacts rating of "III-V" is misleading. Current local consensus (awetstate, Darin McQuoid, cacreeks): Tobin is a genuine **Class IV** run with sieve/tunnel hazards. Explicit quote from awetstate: "Don't come out thinking it is easy class IV." Tobin does not belong on any Class III list.

2. **NF Feather Rock Creek — promoted as the actual Class III NF Feather option.** This is the one NF Feather stretch that is legitimately Class III, explicitly described (awetstate) as "for the class III boater who is tired of the South Fork of the American." Dam-released summer/fall run with one Class IV- rapid you can scout/portage.

3. **Lower Kern — split into two distinct runs (was conflated in v1).**
   - **Jungle Run** (Keysville/Hwy 155 → Miracle Hot Springs, ~6–7 mi): the true Class III Lower Kern. III/III+. Dam-released, summer season. Known for strainers/trees; commercial outfitters and USFS help clear them but low-water years are worse.
   - **Miracle → Democrat** (~12 mi): **Class IV** with a mandatory Class V+ portage at Royal Flush. Not a III run. My v1 "Sandy Flat → Democrat" description corresponded to this section and was incorrectly called a III classic. Corrected.

4. **Mokelumne Electra — downgraded.** AW and Bill Tuthill (cacreeks) both rate the Electra Run as **Class II** at normal flows, II+ to III only above ~1500 cfs. It's the best local beginner/intermediate run for Sacramento-area paddlers, but at your level it's a warmup or a gift to a new boater, not a Class III destination. Keeping it on the list but recharacterizing.

5. **Tuolumne (Meral's Pool → Ward's Ferry) — verified excluded.** I wanted to double-check this because it's often name-checked as a "CA classic," but AW, Whitewater Guidebook, and every commercial source rate it **Class IV+** (sustained IV with IV/IV+ rapids from Rock Garden through Clavey Falls). My v1 was correct to exclude it. Mentioning here because it's a common bucket-list item that doesn't belong on a pure-III list.

**Ratings I checked and did not need to change:** SF American (III+), MF American Oxbow (II-IV with IV Tunnel Chute), Trinity Pigeon Point (III/III+), Upper Sac Box Canyon → Dunsmuir (III-IV), East Fork Carson (II with III pulses / III Gorge above Markleeville), Truckee Floriston (III), Cache Creek Rumsey (III), Klamath Hell's Corner (III+/IV), Kings Banzai (III-IV), Cal Salmon Nordheimer (IV-V, not III).

**Remaining soft spots:** Upper Kern "Limestone" is III-V and is only a III run if you pick-and-choose your sections carefully — I've left it in Tier 2 but with a stronger caveat. Merced El Portal remains genuinely ambiguous (III+ at low flow, IV+ at high flow, contains a Class V drop).

## Methodology

Three parallel approaches, then intersection:

**1. Database enumeration.** Pulled the full California run list from riverfacts.com (~267 entries), which mirrors the Holbek & Stanley canonical catalog. Filtered for any run whose class rating includes III (i.e., "III", "III+", "II-III", "III-IV", "II-IV", "II-V", etc.). Excluded runs rated purely II or IV+/V+.

**2. Editorial curation.** Cross-referenced against:
- Whitewater Guidebook (whitewaterguidebook.com) — editorially selective
- Commercial outfitter rosters (runs that outfitters reliably run commercially are de facto "classics")
- American Whitewater featured runs
- as.csuchico.edu / Outdoor Rec "beginner-to-intermediate" staple lists

**3. Community signal proxy.** Runs that consistently show up in Mountain Buzz / Boater Talk discussion, are staples for Sacramento / Gold Country / Bay Area clubs, or make "if you only do ten" lists in paddling media.

A run earned the **"classic"** label if it scored across at least two of those three dimensions AND is predominantly Class III at normal kayaking flows.

## Refined "classics" — cross-source agreement

Runs appearing in at least two of {commercial outfitter roster, editorial best-of, community staple} AND predominantly III at normal flows.

### Tier 1 — iconic / must-do for any California Class III paddler
1. **South Fork American** — Chili Bar, Coloma-to-Lotus, and The Gorge runs. The single most-paddled III in California (possibly the country). Dam-released, reliable season May–September. Rapids: Meatgrinder, Troublemaker, Satan's Cesspool, Hospital Bar.
2. **Middle Fork American — Oxbow → Drivers Flat/Greenwood** — 17 miles of continuous III with Tunnel Chute (IV) at mile 1 and Kanaka Falls lower. Dam-released, summer-friendly. Gorgeous canyon.
3. **Upper Sacramento — Box Canyon → Sims** — granite pool-drop under Castle Crags. Spring/early summer.
4. **East Fork Carson — Hangman's → Carson River Ranch (overnight)** — II+ with III pulses, hot springs, multi-day wilderness feel. You've done this.
5. **Truckee River (Floriston stretch)** — roadside, reliable, continuous read-and-run III.
6. **Kings River — Garnet Dike → Kirch Flat (Banzai)** — spring-snowmelt Sierra classic. Long-ish drive from Rocklin but worth it.
7. **Klamath — Hell's Corner (Happy Camp stretch)** — big-water III; multi-day option.
8. **Cache Creek — Rumsey Canyon** — Bay Area/Sacramento winter-spring staple.
9. **Trinity — Pigeon Point** — long season (Lewiston release), scenic, pool-drop III.
10. **Lower Kern — "Jungle Run"** (Keysville/Hwy 155 → Miracle Hot Springs, ~6-7 mi). SoCal's summer Class III classic, dam-controlled, reliable flows. Note: the stretch below Miracle Hot Springs (Miracle → Democrat) is IV with a mandatory V+ portage — that's a different run.

### Tier 2 — well-established regional classics
11. **McCloud — Ah-Di-Na (Reservoir → Shasta)** — remote III-IV wilderness.
12. **Cal Salmon — South Fork (Methodist → Forks)** — III section in an otherwise IV-V area.
13. **South Fork Eel — Big Bend → Piercy** — winter/spring II-III, redwoods scenery.
14. **Main Eel — Dos Rios → Alderpoint** — multi-day wilderness, rain-dependent.
15. **NF Feather — Rock Creek section (dam release)** — the genuine Class III NF Feather run. Dam-released summer/fall; one IV- rapid that's easily scouted/portaged. Billed locally as the alternative for III paddlers tired of SF American. *(Tobin and Lobin sections are IV and IV+ respectively — not III.)*
16. **Merced — El Portal → Briceburg** — snowmelt III+/IV depending on CFS; skill-dependent. Contains one Class V (Ned's Gulch).
17. **Upper Kern — Camp 3 / Limestone area selections** — III-V as a full run; there are genuine III segments (Camp 3 area) that are summer staples, but you have to pick-and-choose or risk a IV+ rapid. Not a "put on and read-and-run" III.
18. **Mokelumne — Electra Run** — Class II at normal flows, II+/III only at higher water (>1500 cfs). Closest dam-released run to Rocklin, but it's a warmup/guest run at your level, not a Class III destination. Retained for completeness because it's heavily referenced in the Sac-area scene.

### Tier 3 — local / regional classics (less universal fame)
- **Alameda Creek (Sunol → Niles)** — Bay Area backyard, narrow weather window.
- **NF American — Shirttail (Ponderosa → Mineral Bar)** — shorter, more technical.
- **Arroyo Seco** — obscure Coast Range treat, narrow season.
- **Little Sur River** — tiny coastal classic.
- **Outlet Creek / Tomki Creek** — winter rain runs, Mendocino.
- **Mad River (Ruth stretch)** — NorCal locals' run.
- **Goodwin Canyon (Stanislaus below Goodwin Dam)** — 4 miles of III-IV(V); borderline for III paddlers.

## Confidence notes

### Highest confidence (unambiguous, multi-source agreement)
SF American, MF American (Oxbow), EF Carson overnight, Upper Sac, Truckee, Cache Creek Rumsey, Trinity Pigeon Point, Lower Kern town run, Kings Banzai, Klamath Hell's Corner. These are bedrock.

### Moderate confidence
- **NF Feather classifications are fuzzy** — riverfacts rates several sections III-V, but locals debate the character constantly. Often paddled as IV by competent kayakers.
- **Merced El Portal**: traditionally "III+" at low flows, commercially sold as IV, contains one Class V (Ned's Gulch / Split Rock). Classification drifts significantly with CFS.
- **Upper Kern (Limestone)**: rated III-V; a pure III paddler would be walking or in trouble on the whole run.
- **Klamath** subsections vary — some stretches are II, others III+(IV); the "Hell's Corner" label floats.
- **Goodwin Canyon**: rated III-IV(V) — at high water it's solidly IV; at low water it's accessible to strong III paddlers.

### Low confidence
- Some ratings in the underlying database are derived from Holbek & Stanley editions as old as 1988–1998 and may not reflect current hazards (wood, new strainers, channel changes).
- All ratings are flow-dependent — a III run at medium flow is IV at high water and II at low.
- I did not verify current access / permits / shuttle logistics. Some put-ins have changed; some runs have permit systems now that didn't exist in the reference material.

### Caveats on the method itself
- The riverfacts database is biased toward kayaking-era descents (1980s–1990s). A few more recently discovered or re-evaluated runs may not be captured.
- "Classic" is socially constructed — the shortlist reflects Sacramento/Bay Area/commercial-outfitter weighting. A paddler based in Humboldt or Kern County would construct a different shortlist.
- Commercial dam-released rivers are over-represented vs. snowmelt-dependent ones, because they have stable trip-volume and public visibility.
- I didn't pull actual American Whitewater trip-report counts (that would have given harder "popularity" numbers); I'm using commercial roster + editorial curation as a proxy.

### What I did NOT do
- Current flow windows per run.
- Safety verification (strainers, wood, current hazards) — these change year to year.
- Cross-reconcile with Cassady & Calhoun's ratings (which sometimes differ from Holbek/Stanley).
- Distance/drive time from Rocklin (relevant to you specifically but not done here).
