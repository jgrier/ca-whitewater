# California Class V Whitewater Runs — Comprehensive List & Classics

> **Companion to the Class III and IV documents.** Same methodology, same sources. California is arguably the best Class V destination in the world outside the Himalayas, and the list reflects that — it's long.

## Methodology

Same three-pass approach:

**1. Database enumeration.** Filtered the riverfacts.com / Holbek-Stanley database for any run whose rating includes V: "V", "V+", "IV-V", "IV-V+", "III-V", "II-V", "II-V+", or "(V)" / "(V+)" as a portage-gate. Excluded runs that are rated V only because of one portageable drop in an otherwise-IV run, when that's the local consensus (e.g. Lower Kern Miracle-Democrat, where Royal Flush is portaged as V+ in an otherwise-IV run — belongs on the IV list).

**2. Editorial curation.** Cross-referenced against:
- Whitewater Guidebook (whitewaterguidebook.com)
- Darin McQuoid's rating list (darinmcquoid.com) — the single most authoritative CA-V kayaker reference
- awetstate.com (kayaker-perspective trip reports)
- liquidlore.com, Oregon Kayaking, Paddle California blog
- cacreeks.com (Bill Tuthill)
- American Whitewater river detail pages
- Adventure Sports Journal "The Rivers K" and "Droppin' the Royal Gorge" features

**3. Community signal proxy.** Runs referenced in the V kayaker canon — Cherry Creek Race entrants, the bucket-list articles, the "classic California V" mental model shared among expert paddlers.

A run is a **classic** if cross-source agreement places it predominantly in the V range at normal kayaking flows AND it's consistently run (not just once-descended).

---

## Important: the "V" rating spreads enormously

Unlike III or IV, "Class V in California" covers runs that are wildly different experiences:

- **"Easy V" / "V-"** — Chamberlain Falls at 3500 cfs, Giant Gap at moderate flow, NF Mokelumne Tiger Creek pushed high, Bald Rock at 800 cfs: scoutable, pool-drop, committing but not desperate.
- **"Solid V"** — Cherry Creek at normal flow, Forks of the Kern, Burnt Ranch Gorge, Fordyce, Cal Salmon Nordheimer: V rapids that require real V skills, consequences significant, some mandatory portages.
- **"Stout V / V+"** — Bald Rock, Royal Gorge, Dinkey Creek, Devil's Canyon of MF Feather, Upper Kings Garlic Falls: mandatory portages, big drops, long swims, serious consequences.
- **"Expedition V+" / "Extreme V"** — Middle Kings, Upper Cherry Creek, North Fork of the North Fork American, Royal Gorge Heath Falls section: multi-day, hike-in, elite-only, regularly un-runnable at low/high flows.

I've tagged each classic below with which bucket it falls in.

Two words of definitional honesty: I'm *not* an expert-V kayaker, and a lot of this is aggregating other kayakers' assessments rather than anything I can independently verify. For the harder ends of the spectrum (Royal Gorge, Middle Kings, Upper Cherry) my confidence is lower than for the sport-V classics.

## Refined classics — cross-source agreement

### Tier 1 — iconic California Class V runs (the bucket list)

These are the runs that every CA V kayaker references, and that out-of-state/out-of-country paddlers travel to run. Consensus across McQuoid, Whitewater Guidebook, AW, and commercial rafting.

1. **Cherry Creek / Upper Tuolumne** (Holm PH → Lumsden, ~7 mi). Almost certainly **the most famous V run in the world**. 12 V rapids, the Miracle Mile, Mushroom, Flat Rock Falls, Lumsden Falls. Dam-released mid-July through Labor Day (kayaks into fall). The Cherry Creek Race is a calendar event. *Solid V; V+ at high water.*

2. **Forks of the Kern** (~17 mi, 1–3 days). 40 Class IV rapids with 3 mandatory V stops (Vortex, Westwall, Carson Falls). Wilderness, 2-mile hike-in, Golden Trout country. Snowmelt-dependent. *Solid V with stout V rapids.*

3. **MF Feather — Devil's Canyon** (Nelson Point → Milsap, 32.5 mi, 3–4 days). The canonical CA V expedition. Holbek/Stanley called it "the best self-supported wilderness trip in California"; Cassady/Calhoun called it "the most spectacular and most difficult river in this book." Includes the portage at Granite Dome Falls. *Solid V expedition.*

4. **MF Feather — Bald Rock Canyon** (Milsap → Lake Oroville, 6.5 mi). Granite canyon day-trip classic. Atom Bomb Falls is the mandatory portage; everything else runs. Rated V+ in the books; McQuoid and liquidlore both call it IV+/V in practice. *Stout V/V+.*

5. **Burnt Ranch Gorge (Trinity)** (Cedar Flat → Hawkins Bar, ~10 mi). IV+ with 4 mandatory V rapids (Burnt Ranch Falls series). Dam-released summer/fall (Trinity Lewiston release). *Solid V day trip.*

6. **Cal Salmon — Nordheimer** (Nordheimer CG → Somes Bar, 10.2 mi). IV+ with 3 Vs (Cascade, Last Chance, Freight Train). Spring snowmelt. Often the first "real V" for advancing IV paddlers — but Freight Train is no joke. *Solid V.*

7. **Giant Gap (NF American)** (Euchre Bar → Iowa Hill Bridge, 14.5 mi). Hike-in, deep canyon. cacreeks: "IV+ with 3 V rapids"; AW and locals: "California classic run with great canyon wilderness scenery." Spring snowmelt. *Solid V wilderness day trip.*

8. **Fordyce Creek** (Fordyce Lake → Spaulding, 7–12 mi). Dam-released, high-Sierra granite. Fordyce Falls / In-Your-Face, Split Falls, The Hole that Ate the Donner Party, Knobs. Introduced by AW's flow negotiations. *Solid V; several easy portages available.*

9. **Kaweah — Hospital Rock (MF Kaweah)** (Buck's Flat → below Gateway, ~5 mi). V-Slide (V), Zero to Sixty, granite bedrock and slides. "Cali kayaking at its best" per liquidlore. Short season, Sequoia NP. *Solid V.*

10. **Dinkey Creek — Waterfalls section** (Ross Creek → NF Kings, 6.3 mi, 2 days). Adventure Sports Journal: "Dinkey Creek is arguably the best whitewater run in California." Expedition. *Stout V+ with portages.*

### Tier 2 — well-established V classics

11. **Royal Gorge (NF American)** (16 mi, expedition). Wabena Falls (70 ft), Heath Falls, Scott's Drop, Rattlesnake Falls. First descent Scott Lindgren late '90s. *Expedition V+, elite.*

12. **Upper Kings / Garlic Falls** (SF Kings, Park Boundary → Boyden, ~8 mi). 3.6-mile hike-in, commitment. "Classic class V Sierra whitewater with huge granite rocks, waterfalls, crystal clear water." *Solid V/V+ with portages.*

13. **Middle Kings** (MF Kings, Dusy → SF confluence, 29 mi, multi-day expedition). Widely considered the pinnacle of US expedition kayaking. Airlift in, multi-day, V+. *Extreme expedition V+.*

14. **SF American — Golden Gate** (Peavine → Forebay, ~9 mi). McQuoid rates V-V+. Technical granite, committing, often paired mentally with Bald Rock and Dinkey as "the big three in-state test pieces." *Stout V/V+.*

15. **SF American — Slab Creek** (Slab Creek Dam → White Rock PH, 7 mi). Dam-released. Rock Garden (V), Meat Locker. *Solid V day trip.*

16. **Rubicon (Ellicott Bridge → Ralston)** (20 mi, 2 days). Rare high-water runs only (Hell Hole Dam spills rarely). "More great rapids in 20 miles than any other river I know" — Dan Dunlap. *Solid V wilderness overnight; hard to catch.*

17. **NF Mokelumne — Devil's Nose** (below Salt Springs Dam). AW-coordinated scheduled release (May, target 900 cfs). Short, stout, technical. *Solid V, scheduled.*

18. **South Silver** (SF Silver Creek above Icehouse, 1.5–2 mi). Granite-slide classic. Autobahn, Teacups, Plastic Surgery, Quadruple Bypass. 590 fpm. *V with V+ slides; unique California character.*

19. **NF Stanislaus — Sourgrass / Board's Crossing** (3 mi). Short, stout, spring. McQuoid lists the NF Stan system as V-rated.

20. **MF Stanislaus — Sand Bar → Camp 9** (14 mi). IV-V+; IV at low flow, V at higher. Dam-released with access/permission complexity.

21. **NF Feather — Tobin** (Cresta Dam → Poe Dam, 6.5 mi). **Rating disputed** — McQuoid rates V; awetstate rates IV with V-consequence sieves. Put on your Class V list if you're going by McQuoid; keep it as "serious IV" if you're going by the more commercial lens. Dam-released reliable summer/fall. *IV/V borderline; consequence > difficulty.*

22. **Dry Meadow Creek (The Teacups)** (1.5 mi). Iconic granite cascading teacups entering the Kern at Forks. Photo classic. *V, short, specialized.*

23. **Brush Creek (Kern trib)** (1.5 mi). Falls and slides run — "one of the best class IV creeks in California" per Sierra South — but widely considered V by kayakers. *V creek classic.*

24. **SF Kings — Horseshoe Bend / Boyden Cave → MF** (4.4 mi). McQuoid: V with scenery competitive with Upper Cherry Creek. 2-day commitment. *V+ expedition-style.*

25. **MF Kaweah — Upper (above Hospital Rock)** and **EF Kaweah** — both V/V+; short-season spring runs.

26. **Big Kimshew Creek** — V-V+ per McQuoid; classic NorCal creek.

27. **Butte Creek** — IV-V (P) per McQuoid; a local Chico-area classic.

28. **SF Cal Salmon (Limestone Bluffs section)** — IV-V, short snowmelt window.

29. **SF Smith Gorge and MF Smith Oregon Hole** — Both technically straddle IV+/V; the gorges are V at most flows. Winter rain-dependent.

## Confidence notes

### Highest confidence (unanimous multi-source)
- **Cherry Creek, Forks of the Kern, MF Feather Devil's Canyon, Bald Rock, Burnt Ranch Gorge, Cal Salmon Nordheimer, Giant Gap, Fordyce, Dinkey Creek** — these are the undisputed CA V canon. Every source treats them as classics.
- **Royal Gorge, Middle Kings, Upper Cherry Creek, Garlic Falls** — undisputed at the expedition-V tier.
- **Hospital Rock Kaweah** — consistently rated V; McQuoid, liquidlore, awetstate all agree.

### Moderate confidence — genuine community rating disputes
- **NF Feather Tobin**: McQuoid rates **V**; awetstate rates **IV** with V-consequence sieves; Holbek-Stanley rates **III-V**. These disagreements are not about the whitewater — they're about whether the sieve/tunnel hazards push the rating up. A V-rated paddler should treat it as V; an IV paddler who's ready for consequence may run it. I had it in IV Tier 2 in the companion doc; leaving it here in V Tier 2 with explicit "borderline" flag because both framings are legitimate.
- **Bald Rock**: sources split between V (AW, WG, Holbek-Stanley) and IV+/V (liquidlore, McQuoid "V (P)"). Practical answer: it's a serious run with one mandatory portage, and whether you call it V or V+ doesn't matter if you can't paddle V.
- **SF American Golden Gate** / **Slab Creek**: both rated V but with significant flow dependency and access complexity. McQuoid's "V-V+" for Golden Gate is more authoritative for kayaking than the raft-biased IV-V.
- **Cal Salmon Nordheimer**: outfitters often frame it as "step-up from IV" but it has 3 mandatory Vs and should be treated as V.
- **Upper Kern Limestone at high flow**: 2500+ cfs pushes it into IV+/V. Flow-dependent.

### Low confidence
- **"Extreme V" tier**: I don't personally know enough to rank these. McQuoid's blog and Adventure Sports Journal pieces are my best sources; I'm aggregating their judgments.
- **Rain-dependent Coast Range runs**: Thomes, Putah, Arroyo Mocho, etc. are genuinely run in good water years but sparsely documented.
- **Rating drift over time**: many of these ratings come from Holbek/Stanley (1988–98) and paddling community consensus has evolved. Some runs that were V first-descents are now done by strong IV paddlers in modern boats; some runs have gained V hazards from wood accumulation and channel change.
- **Access/permission/legality**: some of these runs (Kaweah in Sequoia NP, MF Stanislaus, Tuolumne high stretches) have access or legal complexity that may have changed since the cited sources.

### Method-specific caveats (applies to V especially)
- **Paper ratings dramatically under-represent consequence.** A "V" with a bad sieve and no rescue access is worse than a "V+" with scoutable pool-drops. Ratings don't capture this; local knowledge does.
- **Kayaker vs rafter ratings diverge more at V.** Commercial outfitters rate the Forks of the Kern as "V" and Cherry Creek as "V"; kayakers see these as very different animals. I've used kayaker ratings where available.
- **"Classic" in V context means "consistently run by competent V paddlers"** — it does NOT mean "must-do for every paddler." Half the Tier 1 list requires skills that take years to build.

### What I did not do
- Compare to Cassady & Calhoun on every run (only excerpts).
- Verify current flow/release schedules.
- Distinguish modern whitewater boat vs old-school creeker rating drift systematically.
- Evaluate the Middle Kings / Royal Gorge extreme-V tier meaningfully — that's beyond what I can responsibly assess from sources.
- Rank by proximity to Rocklin (though several Tier 1/2 runs sit within 2–4 hours: MF American Oxbow Tunnel Chute, NF American Giant Gap/Chamberlain, SF American Slab Creek/Golden Gate, Rubicon, Fordyce, SF Yuba/Fordyce system, NF Feather Tobin, NF Mokelumne Devil's Nose).

## Offers to drill deeper

- **Proximity-sorted V runs from Rocklin** — you're well-placed for the Yuba/American/Feather/Mokelumne V scene specifically; many of these are 2–3 hour drives.
- **V dam-release calendar** — which releases happen when (Fordyce, Devil's Nose, Tobin, Slab Creek, Goodwin, etc.).
- **"Progression toward V" list** — ordered IV+ → easy V → solid V → stout V, if that's useful.
- **Expedition logistics** — MF Feather, Middle Kings, Rubicon, Upper Cherry, Royal Gorge each have specific access/permit/shuttle considerations that would take dedicated research.
- **Wood/hazard update pass** — since many of these runs change hazards year to year, it would be worth a dedicated search for recent trip reports on any specific run before running it.
