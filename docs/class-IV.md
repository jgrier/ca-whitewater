# California Class IV Whitewater Runs — Comprehensive List & Classics

> **Companion to the Class III document.** Same methodology, same sources, same confidence-scoring approach. Cross-references already built in (v1 of this doc incorporates the corrections that took v1→v2 of the Class III doc).

## Methodology

Same three-pass approach as the Class III document:

**1. Database enumeration.** Filtered the riverfacts.com / Holbek-Stanley database (~267 CA runs) for any run whose rating includes IV: "IV", "IV+", "III-IV", "IV-V", "II-IV", "II-V", "III-V" (where IV is the modal character). Excluded runs rated purely III, and excluded runs rated IV-V where the IV is incidental to a predominantly V character (Cherry Creek, Forks of the Kern, Burnt Ranch Gorge, Generation Gap are all called out separately below — they're "IV+ with Vs" not IV runs).

**2. Editorial curation.** Cross-referenced against:
- Whitewater Guidebook (whitewaterguidebook.com)
- cacreeks.com (Bill Tuthill, explicit kayaking perspective)
- awetstate.com (kayaker-perspective trip reports)
- Darin McQuoid and liquidlore.com (advanced kayaker perspective)
- American Whitewater river detail pages
- Dreamflows cross-listing
- Commercial outfitter IV offerings (Momentum, OARS, Whitewater Voyages, etc.)

**3. Community signal proxy.** Runs that consistently appear in Mountain Buzz / BoaterTalk discussion, local kayaker blog write-ups, and in the "California IV progression" that advanced paddlers reference.

A run is a **classic** if it scores across at least two dimensions AND is predominantly Class IV at normal kayaking flows.

One important definitional note: California Class IV covers a huge range. There's a big difference between:
- **"Sport IV"** — Chamberlain Falls, MF American Tunnel Chute, Hospital Bar at 4000 cfs: pool-drop, scoutable, easy swims
- **"Committing IV"** — NF Smith, SF Smith Gorge, Hospital Rock on Kaweah: remote, continuous, bad swim consequences
- **"Creeky IV"** — Goodwin Canyon, Tobin on NF Feather, NF Yuba Goodyears: technical, sieve-prone, wood-prone
- **"Big-water IV"** — Tuolumne Main, Cal Salmon Nordheimer, Lower Kern Miracle-to-Democrat: high-volume, pushy, IV+ features

I've flagged which bucket each classic falls into. A Chamberlain Falls regular is not automatically ready for a NF Smith or Hospital Rock.

## Runs I excluded from "IV classics" because they're really V

Several runs often name-checked in California IV contexts are genuinely V and belong on a Class V list, not here:

- **Forks of the Kern** — 40 IV rapids but **3 stout V**s (Vortex, Westwall, Carson Falls). The National Park Service, recreation.gov, and all commercial outfitters rate it **Class V**. Advanced IV paddlers reference it aspirationally, but it's not a IV run.
- **Burnt Ranch Gorge (Trinity)** — cacreeks rates "IV+ with 4 class V." Commercial outfitters rate it **V**. The V rapids (Burnt Ranch Falls series) gate the run.
- **Cal Salmon Nordheimer** — cacreeks: "IV+ with 3 class V rapids." Whitewater Guidebook rates it **Class V**. 3 mandatory Vs (Cascade, Last Chance, Freight Train) make it a V trip — though often pitched as an "intro to V rowing" or strong-IV step-up in kayaks.
- **Cherry Creek (Upper T)** — unambiguously **V**.
- **NF American Generation Gap** — **V** (above Giant Gap).
- **Kaweah Hospital Rock** — properly **V+** (V-Slide, Zero to Sixty) — locals call the IV-V run "the Gateway/Dinely" or "Upper 6," which is genuinely IV with an optional V.
- **MF Feather Bald Rock Canyon** — **V+**, not IV.
- **SF American Slab Creek** — rated IV-V but the "Rock Garden" and other features push it into V territory at most flows per local consensus.

The borderline cases where kayaker practice differs from written ratings: NF Yuba Goodyears stretch, NF Feather Tobin/Lobin, SF Smith Gorge, Merced El Portal at high water. I've kept these in Tier 2/3 with flow caveats rather than push them to V.

## Refined "classics" — cross-source agreement

### Tier 1 — iconic, must-do California Class IV runs
1. **NF American — Chamberlain Falls** (Mineral Bar → Yankee Jim's Bridge, ~5 mi). The canonical CA "first Class IV." Pool-drop, 5–6 Class IV rapids (Slaughter's Sluice, Chamberlain Falls IV+, Tongue & Groove, Zig-Zag, Achilles Heel, Bogus Thunder, Staircase). Spring/early-summer snowmelt-dependent. Most-run Class IV in CA.

2. **MF American — Oxbow → Drivers Flat / Greenwood** (~17 mi). Tunnel Chute (IV) and Kanaka Falls (IV+) headline; most of the run is strong III with Ruck-a-Chucky portage at the bottom. Dam-released, summer-friendly. The "Class III+ paddler's step-up" in Northern CA, and a genuine IV overall.

3. **Tuolumne Main (Meral's Pool → Ward's Ferry)** (~18 mi). **The** California Class IV overnight. Sustained IV from the first rapid (Rock Garden, Nemesis, Sunderland's, Hackamack, Ram's Head) to Clavey Falls (IV+) and below. Dam-released, permit required, world-class wilderness canyon. OARS calls this "the quintessential Class IV run in California."

4. **Upper Kern — "Thunder Run" / Limestone** (Johnsondale → Chamise / PH #3, ~17 mi). Summer dam-released. Class IV at 1500–2500 cfs, IV+ above. Limestone, Joe's Diner, Cable, Buzzard's Perch. SoCal's summer IV classic.

5. **Lower Kern — Miracle Hot Springs → Democrat Dam** (~12 mi). Dam-released, reliable summer. Dead Man's Curve (IV), Horseshoe Falls (IV), with mandatory **V+ portage at Royal Flush**. Great IV kayaking with one committed portage. Note this is distinct from the "Jungle Run" above it, which is the III Lower Kern.

6. **Kaweah — "Upper 6" / Gateway → Dinely (aka "The Gateway")** (~3 mi, often combined with the "Lower 4" for a full-day). Gateway, Chute, Osterizer, Willows, Suicide Falls, Slicky. Classic technical IV boulder-garden; snowmelt spring run. Part of the "Rivers K" (Kings/Kaweah/Kern) trifecta.

7. **Kings — Banzai (Garnet Dike → Kirch Flat)** (~9.5 mi). Spring snowmelt in a big, warm canyon. Fang Tooth (IV+), Mule Rock (IV), Banzai (IV+). Long season in a good water year.

8. **Upper Sac — Box Canyon → Sims → Gibson** sequence. The three sections together form a full IV run under Castle Crags. Box Canyon is III-IV, Dunsmuir-to-Sims is III-IV, Castle Craig-to-Sims and Sims-to-Gibson are solid IV. Pool-drop granite, spring/early-summer.

9. **Goodwin Canyon (Stanislaus below Goodwin Dam)** (~4 mi, run twice). "Mr. Toad's Wild Ride" IV+ headline rapid; Impaler, Haunted House, Matterhorn. Early spring / fall releases. Short but intense — compact, runnable multiple times in a day. Whitewater Voyages markets it as Class IV+.

10. **Cache Creek / Trinity / Klamath Hell's Corner at high water** — these are the big-water IV options. Hell's Corner at high flow is a genuine IV (rather than III+); Trinity Burnt Ranch Gorge's upper approach stretches can be paddled as IV with scouts. Included here more as "high-water IV classic day trips" than must-do runs.

### Tier 2 — well-established regional classics
11. **NF Smith (Low Divide → Gasquet)** — wilderness IV with IV+ at higher flow. One of California's most scenic runs; serpentine canyon, The Grotto, waterfalls. Rain-dependent winter/spring.

12. **SF Smith Gorge (Craig's Beach area)** — ~1-mile gorge with Good Luck (IV+/V), The Boof (IV+), The Slot (IV), Final Rapid (IV). Can run the surrounding III-IV stretch with the gorge as the crux.

13. **MF Smith — Oregon Hole Gorge** — IV+/V, 2.3 mi. Often paired with SF Gorge in a Smith weekend.

14. **NF Yuba — Goodyears Bar → Hwy 49** (~8.5 mi). Spring snowmelt IV, technical and sometimes woody. Downieville locals' classic.

15. **SF American — Slab Creek (Slab Creek Dam → White Rock PH)** — IV-V dam-released, highly local, big slides, Rock Garden.

16. **NF Feather — Tobin / Lobin (Cresta Dam → Poe Dam)** — Tobin is IV (with sieve hazards that the 1998 Holbek-Stanley rating of III-V obscures); Lobin is IV+. Dam-released, reliable summer/fall. *Do not mistake for the III Rock Creek section upstream.*

17. **Pit 5 (Pit 5 Dam → Pit 5 PH)** (~9 mi) — scheduled release IV, big-volume basalt canyon, near Big Bend.

18. **Pit Falls (Pit 1)** (~7 mi) — scheduled release, IV-V character with the named "Pit River Falls" (6–8 ft ledge).

19. **Merced El Portal → Briceburg** — snowmelt, III+ at low flow, IV+ at high flow, includes the Class V Ned's Gulch (mandatory scout; some parties portage). Full-season classic; pick your flow.

20. **SF Yuba — Bridgeport Gorge (Hwy 49 → Bridgeport)** — IV-V, ~7 mi, technical slotty spring run.

21. **MF Stanislaus — Camp 9** (Sand Bar Flat → Camp 9 PH) — IV-V dam-released, big slides, early season.

22. **NF Stanislaus — Board's Crossing → Calaveras Big Trees** — spring snowmelt IV-V, short but stout.

23. **Upper McCloud (Fowler → Reservoir)** — III-IV, remote, beautiful. Shorter season.

24. **NF Mokelumne — Tiger Creek** — AW-coordinated scheduled releases (~700 cfs), Class IV, 14 mi. Spring release dates.

## Confidence notes

### Highest confidence (multi-source unanimous)
- **Tuolumne Main** — every source agrees IV/IV+. Bucket-list CA IV.
- **Chamberlain Falls** — universally "the" intro CA IV. awetstate: "classic class III+/IV- at low flows...to IV (at flows greater than 900)".
- **MF American Oxbow** — commercially run as IV; cacreeks/AW/outfitters agree.
- **Goodwin Canyon** — Whitewater Voyages (IV+), AO Rafting (IV+), consistent ratings.
- **Lower Kern Miracle-to-Democrat** — universal IV with V+ portage.
- **Kaweah Gateway/Dinely** — IV/IV+ agreed (KWA, awetstate, Whitewater Guidebook).
- **NF Smith, SF Smith Gorge, MF Smith Oregon Hole** — all unambiguous IV-to-IV+ runs.
- **Upper Sac stretches** — IV per riverfacts and consistent with commercial offerings.

### Moderate confidence — flow-sensitive or contested
- **Upper Kern Limestone**: per Sierra South, "below 1500 CFS many boaters consider the run to be about class III+ or IV-. In the 1500-2500 range, it's about class IV, increasing to IV+ above about 2500 CFS." So it's a IV run in the commonly-paddled flow window but not always.
- **Merced El Portal**: genuinely ambiguous; contains a Class V rapid (Ned's Gulch). The "IV classic" framing is true at moderate flow and stretches up to IV+/V at high flow.
- **Kings Banzai**: IV classic at spring flows but mostly III at lower flows.
- **NF Feather Tobin/Lobin**: Holbek-Stanley's old III-V rating is misleading; awetstate is explicit that Tobin is IV and Lobin is IV+, with sieve hazards that make a swim consequential. Don't underrate.
- **Klamath Hell's Corner**: III+ at normal flow, IV at high (~2500+ cfs through the turbines). Categorization depends on when you run it.
- **NF Yuba Goodyears**: classic but wood-prone; ratings drift with each winter.

### Low confidence — heavily flow-dependent or sparsely documented
- **Pit Falls (Pit 1)** and **Pit 5** — depend entirely on scheduled PG&E releases and whether you catch 1500+ cfs day. Outside release windows they're different rivers.
- **MF Stanislaus Camp 9** — access permissions and PG&E release schedule change frequently.
- **Clear Creek, Bear Creek, Sespe, Arroyo Valle** — rain-dependent; some years they just don't come in. Ratings in database may predate significant channel/wood changes.
- **SF American Slab Creek** — access/release issues; I don't have confident current beta.
- **South Coast / SoCal runs** — generally less paddled, less up-to-date info.

### Caveats on the method
Same as Class III doc, plus two IV-specific ones:
- **The gap between kayak and raft IV ratings is bigger.** Rafters treat "IV" as a commercial category with guides; kayakers self-rescue. Commercial "IV" offerings may be kayaker III+.
- **Sieves, wood, and entrapment** matter enormously at IV in California — notably NF American Chamberlain (entrapment deaths at low water), NF Feather Tobin (sieves), NF Yuba (wood). Current hazards dominate the paper rating.

### What I did NOT do
- Pull AW gauge readings, current wood reports, or permit status for any specific run.
- Filter by proximity to Rocklin (you're sitting on the best IV region in the state — NF American, MF American, Upper Sac, Pit, NF Feather Tobin, NF Yuba Goodyears, and Goodwin are all within 3–4 hours).
- Reconcile with Cassady & Calhoun's ratings for each run (only excerpt checks).
- Differentiate seasonal flow windows.
