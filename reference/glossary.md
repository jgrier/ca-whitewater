# Glossary

Terms used throughout the project and docs.

## Difficulty ratings

Based on the International Scale of River Difficulty, Class I–VI.

- **Class I** — Easy. Fast water, small waves. Few obstructions, easy self-rescue.
- **Class II** — Novice. Straightforward rapids, clear channels.
- **Class III** — Intermediate. Moderate rapids, irregular waves, requires maneuvering. Strong eddies, swifter current. Scouting often advisable for inexperienced paddlers.
- **Class IV** — Advanced. Powerful, predictable rapids requiring precise boat handling. May feature large unavoidable waves and holes. Scouting often necessary. Rescue difficult.
- **Class V** — Expert. Long, obstructed, violent rapids. Exposed to significant hazards. Scouting mandatory. Rescue difficult even for experts.
- **Class V+** — Pushing the limit of the current scale. More committing, more consequence.
- **Class VI** — Extreme and exploratory. Rarely attempted. Errors have catastrophic consequences.

## Rating qualifier notation

Ratings in the database preserve the original source's format. These conventions:

- **III+** — harder than III, not quite IV
- **IV-** — easier than IV, harder than III+
- **III-IV** — range; typically IV at normal flow, III at low flow, or mix of both
- **III-V** — broad range; usually means a mostly-III or IV run with a V rapid or two
- **IV(V)** — IV with one V rapid (often portaged)
- **III(IV)** — III with one IV rapid
- **IV+(V+)** — IV+ with a V+ portage
- **(P)** — portage
- **III-V(V+)** — range III to V with occasional V+

Preserve these qualifiers — they carry meaning that a normalized numeric score would lose.

## River/water terms

- **CFS** (cubic feet per second) — flow rate, the standard US unit for river flow
- **Gauge** — a USGS or local measurement station; CFS readings at specific points. Use the correct gauge for the specific run — a gauge 50 miles downstream may not reflect put-in flow.
- **Dam-released** — flow controlled by a reservoir release rather than natural precipitation. Usually predictable seasonally.
- **Snowmelt** — flow driven by Sierra snowpack; concentrated April–July in most years
- **Rain-dependent** / **free-flowing** — flow depends entirely on rainfall and/or natural runoff. More variable; generally winter-spring for CA coastal/coast-range rivers.
- **Gradient** — vertical drop per horizontal distance, expressed as ft/mi (fpm). Higher gradient generally means steeper / harder whitewater, but not always (big-water runs have big rapids at low gradient).

## Whitewater features

- **Rapid** — any section of accelerated, broken water
- **Pool-drop** — rapid followed by calm pool, repeating. Easier to rescue in.
- **Continuous** — rapids run into each other without significant pools. Harder to recover from a swim.
- **Boulder garden** — rapid with exposed boulders requiring navigation
- **Eddy** — calm water behind an obstruction; used for rest and scouting
- **Horizon line** — visual cue that water drops away; warning to scout
- **Hole** (hydraulic, keeper) — recirculating water at the base of a drop. Can trap paddlers/boats.
- **Sieve** — water flowing through/between rocks where a body can't pass. Lethal.
- **Strainer** — obstruction (usually wood) that water passes through but solid objects can't. Lethal.
- **Undercut** — rock with current flowing under it. Can trap paddlers.
- **Boof** — a technique and a line; paddling over a drop with the bow up to avoid a hole
- **Portage** — walking around a rapid with the boat

## Trip format terms

- **Day run** — paddled in one day
- **Overnight** / **multi-day** — requires camping
- **Expedition** — multi-day wilderness with significant access challenges (hike-in, remote)
- **Self-support** — paddling with camping gear in your boat
- **Shuttle** — driving vehicles between put-in and take-out so you have a car at each end

## CA-specific shorthand

- **C-to-G** — Coloma to Greenwood, the common 20-mile full SF American trip combining Chili Bar + Gorge
- **"The T"** — Tuolumne (Meral's Pool to Ward's Ferry)
- **"The Gap"** — Giant Gap on the NF American
- **"Chamberlain"** — the Class IV NF American run (Mineral Bar to Yankee Jim's)
- **"The Gorge"** — the lower 5 miles of SF American; also used for SF Smith Gorge, Bridgeport Gorge, etc. — context-dependent
- **"Miracle Mile"** — the crux mile of Cherry Creek / Upper Tuolumne
- **"The Rivers K"** — Kings + Kaweah + Kern (Haven Livingston's framing in Adventure Sports Journal)

## Paddler terms

- **Sport boat / river runner** — modern kayak designed for running rivers, not playing
- **Creek boat** — shorter, higher-volume kayak designed for steep technical water
- **Playboat / rodeo boat** — short boat designed for freestyle in holes and waves
- **IK** — inflatable kayak (not the same as a hardshell kayak; flow minimums differ)
- **R1** — single person rowing/paddling a raft
- **Read and run** — paddling a rapid without scouting, because you can see the line from upstream
- **Scout** — stopping to walk down and look at a rapid before running
- **Ghost boat** — intentionally sending an empty boat down a rapid (usually at a portage)
